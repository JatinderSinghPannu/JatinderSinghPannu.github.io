Profile

I am a mathematician and researcher with a strong interest in fluid dynamics, scientific computing, and engineering-led problem solving. My work brings together analytical theory, numerical modelling, and practical teamwork through both academic research and extracurricular projects.

After graduating with a first-class MMath degree in 2023, I began my PhD at Keele University, where I research the spreading and solidification of molten fluid flows. My work combines mathematical modelling, scientific computing, and analytical theory, with a particular focus on thin-film flows and temperature-dependent behaviour. Alongside my academic research, I lead a busy professional and extracurricular life as the aerodynamics sub-team lead for Keele’s MACH2026 team and as a PR manager for two busy Specsavers stores in Cheylesmore and Canley.

Academic / PhD Research

My PhD research focuses on mathematical modelling and scientific computing for molten fluid flows, with particular emphasis on thin-film theory, heat transport, and solidification.

Research area

Thin-film flows

I model fluids where the spreading length scale is much larger than the fluid height, allowing reduced equations for free-surface evolution. I am currently interested in modelling lava and other molten fluid flows.

Methods

Scientific computing

I use finite-difference algorithms to solve complex coupled PDE systems numerically. Working mainly in Python, I use high-performance computing on Keele’s Green HPC to obtain and analyse these solutions.

  • Current focus
    What am I working on? I am currently studying how molten and highly viscous fluids spread, cool, and solidify. This involves deriving and solving coupled free-surface and temperature equations, and investigating how heat transport influences the overall flow dynamics.
  • Tools
    Software and workflow My work uses Python, NumPy, SciPy, numerical methods for PDEs, data visualisation, and cluster computing. I also work with custom finite-difference solvers and high-resolution simulations on HPC systems.
  • Why it matters
    Broader value Understanding how thin molten films spread and cool is important in both natural and industrial settings. This research helps build mathematical models that can explain complex physical behaviour, including phenomena relevant to lava-flow hazard assessment and the mitigation of nuclear power plant reactor meltdowns, where radioactive corium may be spread onto an under-cooled core catcher.
